# Evidence log - IVIVE

"OSP #n" refers to issues in Open-Systems-Pharmacology/OSP-based-publications-and-content
(label: IVIVE); all 14 issues in that label were read. Certara entries are publications,
posters and blogs on certara.com. Paraphrased throughout.

## Scaling chain and systematic under-prediction

- OSP #764, #740, #731 - IVIVE applications and scaling-factor treatments; support the
  multiplicative-chain framing and the practice of ranking chain terms by uncertainty.
- OSP #533, #524 - cases where the bottom-up prediction required an empirical correction;
  basis for the rule that such corrections are compound-and-system-specific and must not
  propagate.
- OSP #456, #426, #425 - IVIVE in the context of altered physiology (including the
  bexarotene case, which also appears in the renal set); demonstrates that a scaling
  factor validated in health does not automatically hold in disease.
- OSP #416, #415, #394 - system-dependent scaling (microsomes vs hepatocytes vs
  recombinant), and the consequences of assay sensitivity limits for low-clearance
  compounds.
- OSP #338, #315, #262 - non-CYP and extrahepatic routes, and permeability-limited
  organ models.

## Transporter IVIVE

- Certara publication, A Mechanistic Framework for In Vitro-In Vivo Extrapolation of
  Liver Membrane Transporters (rosuvastatin-cyclosporine). Source of the requirement to
  separate passive from active permeation and to model the liver as permeability-limited
  when uptake is rate-determining, and of the point that enzyme-transporter interplay is
  governed jointly by system parameters (perfusion) and drug attributes (binding,
  ionisation, lipid partitioning, relative passive versus active permeation).
- Certara publication, Interlaboratory Variability in the Madin-Darby Canine Kidney Cell
  Proteome. Basis for the caution that transporter scaling factors do not transfer
  between laboratories without qualification.

## Bottom-up versus empirical scaling

- Certara publication, Prediction of Metabolic Drug Clearance in Humans: In Vitro-In Vivo
  Extrapolation vs Allometric Scaling (Shiran, Proctor, Howgate, Rowland Yeo, Tucker,
  Rostami-Hodjegan, 2006). 15 CYP-cleared drugs; IVIVE accurate (mean fold error at or
  below 2) in 14 of 15 cases with a fold-error range of about 1.02 to 4.00; five
  allometric variants accurate in 13, 11, 10, 10 and 14 cases respectively, with some
  errors exceeding fivefold. Conclusion adopted here: mechanistic scaling is the more
  reliable default for CYP-cleared drugs, and empirical scaling fails without bound.

## In vitro assay integrity

- Certara blog, What are the most common in vitro drug-drug interaction study gaps?
  (Rioux, Heller, Colombo, 2025). Although framed for DDI, its list is the general
  in vitro integrity checklist used here: incubational binding correction, blood-to-plasma
  ratio, solubility in assay medium, cytotoxicity, recovery, stability, non-specific
  binding, concentration range, positive control sensitivity.

## Limits of confidence outside CYP

- Certara blog, Minimizing risk in hepatic impairment studies (Barter, Wood, 2026).
  Source of the observation that non-CYP system parameters are increasingly characterised
  in the literature but validated probe substrates remain scarce - the example given is
  that only two clinical studies were available to validate UGT1A1 predictions, using
  raltegravir. Adopted here as the general warning that confidence outside CYP3A4 is
  structurally lower and should be stated.
